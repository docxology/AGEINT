#### Lesson 1: SIGINT legal-authority and communications-security governance exercise

**Concept.** **SIGINT legal-authority and communications-security governance exercise** frames SIGINT as authority-bound collection with minimization, handling rules, and communications-security implications.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** ELINT concerns non-communications electromagnetic signals, while a measured feature set and an identification of a particular system are separate claims. Frequency, timing or amplitude patterns can overlap across emitters and depend on sensor calibration, sampling and observation geometry. A classroom label is a hypothesis about supplied synthetic measurements, not evidence of a real weapons system, operator or location; no reception or live collection is authorized.

**Why it matters.** Without explicit treatment of **SIGINT legal-authority**, opportunistic collection undermines requirements decomposition and source-discipline fit review; the lesson builds the habit to separate collection discipline concepts from operational recruitment, interception, or tasking procedures.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** SDR moves some radio-processing functions into software; it does not remove the limits of hardware bandwidth, calibration or sampling. In a completed synthetic table, bins 1–4 contain powers [2, 2, 8, 2] arbitrary units and a supplied comparison rule flags values above 5; the transformation flags bin 3 only. A calibration row adds a +6-unit instrument offset to bin 3, so offset correction yields [2, 2, 2, 2] and withdraws the apparent anomaly; without that row the cause is unknown, not an identified emitter. Duplicating the same table is no corroboration, and removing calibration stops attribution; the attached software-assurance sources do not validate SDR performance, and all work is arithmetic on fictional records, without hardware, reception or transmission.
