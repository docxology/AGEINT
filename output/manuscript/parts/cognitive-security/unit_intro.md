### cognitive resilience and epistemic integrity practice artifact: recurring packet and retained evidence

**Navigation.** Parent section: [@sec:part-cognitive-security].

The recurring practice artifact is a **epistemic-integrity review packet** that draws on provenance chain, correction option, harm note, and confidence language. The unit keeps its learning spine explicit. Classroom packet: a synthetic seminar summary turns 'evidence is inconclusive' into 'the method works'; two later summaries copy it, and the original excerpt remains available.

1. Underline the proposition and uncertainty qualifier in each version, then trace the copying chain.
2. Compare changed wording with the original and separate observed confidence inflation from unknown author intent.
3. Remove the copied summaries as a control: repetition must not strengthen the efficacy claim. Restore the original qualifier and check that the conclusion changes.
4. Submit an epistemic-integrity packet rejecting 'works' as unsupported, preserving the inconclusive result and listing the affected derivative notes for correction. Neither spread nor fluent wording proves malicious intent.
Stop use of the inflated claim in the classroom brief; hand a visible correction and version list to the instructor. Do not infer a clinical condition or profile readers.

### cognitive resilience and epistemic integrity safety boundary: accountable, synthetic, and evidence-bounded limits

No manipulation, covert persuasion, medicalized claims, or population-level targeting.

The full source-lane and evidence-package ledgers appear in the orientation and appendices; this unit introduction keeps only the learner-facing spine for [@ageint238]; [@ageint239].

A missing or unknown gate pauses the affected activity, including processing or sharing already supplied data. A written risk exception cannot waive authority or rights. ## Cognitive-security models: source scope before defensive use

The CCDCOE research summary frames cognitive warfare as pressure on the structures through which a social system interprets evidence, establishes trust, and coordinates decisions. Its systemic invariants include epistemic frameworks, values, collective identity, trust architectures, and expectations about the future. This is a conceptual account, not evidence that disagreement, distress, or institutional failure was caused by a cognitive attack. The unit map [@fig:ageint-cognitive-attack-layers] separates those source concepts from AGEINT's proposed defensive checks; it does not attribute a biological/psychological/social attack taxonomy to that source [@scholarly_ccdcoe_cognitive_warfare_reconception].

For a classroom case, first record the observable artifact and its origin, then compare ordinary error, ambiguity, missing context, and deliberate interference as live alternatives. A defender may check provenance, preserve dissent, and clarify decision ownership without profiling an audience or inferring an adversary. Assign the next check to the alternative it would discriminate; if the evidence cannot distinguish them, retain an unresolved judgment. These are instructional safeguards, not a claim of demonstrated protection.

DARPA's Intrinsic Cognitive Security program is a separate, mixed-reality research effort aiming to develop and validate formal, model-scoped mitigation guarantees. A program goal does not establish a guarantee for an LLM, a human population, or this curriculum. Keep mixed-reality cognition, social-system theory, and agent-software security distinct when borrowing an analogy [@official_darpa_intrinsic_cognitive_security].
