#### Lesson 1: Information, Reporting, Finished Judgment, and Decision Support

**Concept.** **Information, Reporting, Finished Judgment, and Decision Support** applies structured analytic methods with explicit alternatives, evidence tables, and calibrated confidence—not rhetorical certainty.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** Information is recorded content; reporting communicates observations or claims; analysis evaluates their basis and implications; finished intelligence presents bounded judgments to a decision-maker, who remains responsible for action. **Completed synthetic case:** bulletin B1 at 08:00 forecasts severe rain; fictional depot log L1 at 08:10 records water at one entrance. The evaluated observation is one affected entrance, not a closed depot; the assessment is that access may be disrupted, with low confidence about duration, and the product asks the logistics decision-owner to review available alternatives rather than announcing closure. At 08:30 correction L2 says the water was from a pipe: preserve L1, withdraw the storm-causation inference, and stop reusing the superseded brief; this exercises [@sec:analytic-workflow], not a measured forecast method. The inherited STIX/TAXII/ATLAS citations are interoperability context, not evidence for these definitions; the official IC explanation supports the raw/finished distinction [@official_intelligence_gov_how_ic_works].

**Why it matters.** Analysts use **Information, Reporting, Finished Judgment, and Decision Support** to separate intelligence work from ordinary research by naming authority, audience, caveats, and feedback. A defensible treatment names the judgment it enables for cycle mapping and audience-aware product design review, the proof limit that collapsing reporting, inference, and judgment into one line would otherwise hide, and the reviewer accountable for challenge.
