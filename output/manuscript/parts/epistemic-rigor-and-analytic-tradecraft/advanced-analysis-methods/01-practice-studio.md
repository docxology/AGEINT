#### Lesson 1: Red Cell Analysis and Adversarial Wargaming

**Concept.** **Red Cell Analysis and Adversarial Wargaming** uses red-cell review to surface disconfirming evidence and assumptions before dissemination.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** A red cell tests an assessment from an explicitly assigned alternative perspective; a tabletop game explores conditional decisions and consequences under rules. Neither produces evidence of an actual adversary's intentions or a forecast simply because a narrative is coherent. In a paper-only fictional archive exercise, challenge the favored explanation of a delayed catalogue with routine review, transcription error, and missing records; stop before deriving live exploitation, persuasion, or action plans. Inherited AI-security citations are context, not evidence that a red-cell method is accurate [@official_cia_tradecraft_primer].

**Why it matters.** **Red Cell Analysis and Adversarial Wargaming** matters in the **Analytic Tradecraft and Source Integrity** lane because structured reasoning and source-integrity evidence must stay separate from judgment; collapsing reporting, inference, and judgment into one line is a common failure.

#### Lesson 2: Network Analysis: Link Charts, Activity Matrices, Social Network Analysis

#### Lesson 3: Timeline Analysis and Event Sequencing

**Concept.** **Timeline Analysis and Event Sequencing** — Sequence events with source timestamps, uncertainty bands, and alternative orderings before inferring causality.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** **Completed synthetic chronology.** Card A reports at 09:00 that a fictional catalogue was approved at 08:30; B is a 09:10 copy; C reports at 10:00 that approval is still pending but has no event timestamp. Separate event time, report time, timezone, source version, and unknown intervals: C cannot be placed after the approval as an event merely because it was reported later. The initial conclusion is an unresolved status conflict, not that approval was reversed or deliberately concealed. A supplied correction C2 says C described 08:00, removing the conflict and yielding 'pending at 08:00, approved at 08:30'; causal reasons remain unknown. Temporal order alone cannot establish why approval occurred [@official_odni_icd_203; @ageint266].

**Why it matters.** Analysts use **Timeline Analysis and Event Sequencing** to separate reporting from inference, and inference from judgment. A defensible treatment names the judgment it enables for structured reasoning and source-integrity review, the proof limit that collapsing reporting, inference, and judgment into one line would otherwise hide, and the reviewer accountable for challenge.

#### Lesson 5: Writing Intelligence Products: Assessments, Estimates, Warnings

**Concept.** **Writing Intelligence Products: Assessments, Estimates, Warnings** — Draft intelligence products with analytic line, caveats, audience, and dissemination marks.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** An assessment interprets present/past evidence; an estimate addresses future or otherwise uncertain conditions; a warning highlights a consequential possibility, indicators, and decision timing without pretending certainty. A completed fictional assessment reads: 'Approval at 08:30 is supported after C2 corrected the event time; publication by noon remains uncertain; newsletter and digest are dependent copies, and no inference about intent follows.' A warning could identify a missed scheduled publication slot, but must include its expected observation opportunity and benign alternatives. The product's polish is not its evidence [@official_odni_icd_203; @official_odni_icd_206].

#### Lesson 6: Communicating Uncertainty: Forecasts, Confidence, and Calibration

**Concept.** **Communicating Uncertainty: Forecasts, Confidence, and Calibration** — Separate raw reporting from assessed analytic line with explicit confidence and dissent.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** **Completed synthetic forecast.** At 09:00 register 'catalogue published before 12:00 today', resolve only from the supplied authorized publication registry, and assign p=0.60 with low analytic confidence because scheduling evidence is incomplete. At 10:00 a supplied delay card changes p to 0.30 without erasing the first forecast; the 11:30 registry card resolves y=1, giving the final forecast a Brier score (0.30−1)²=0.49. For a separate invented batch p=(0.75,0.75,0.25,0.25), y=(1,0,1,0), mean Brier=0.3125 versus 0.25 for constant 0.50; both probability bins resolve at 0.50, illustrating miscalibration in this tiny batch, not estimating enduring skill. Proper scores reflect calibration and other aspects of forecast quality; one outcome cannot establish calibration. Likelihood concerns the event, confidence concerns the evidential basis: 'likely, low confidence' and 'unlikely, high confidence' are coherent combinations; none is a causal effect estimate [@official_odni_icd_203].

#### Lesson 7: Machine-Assisted Analysis: Automation, Independent Review, and Abstention

**Concept.** **Machine-Assisted Analysis: Automation, Independent Review, and Abstention** — Decide automation boundaries with logging, evaluation, and mandatory human review gates.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** Automate bounded extraction or duplicate grouping only with traceable source spans; retain human responsibility for framing, ambiguous timestamps, alternative explanations, and release decisions. In a supplied fictional grading record, reviewer R1 awards 2/3 for independence handling and R2 awards 3/3 because the model calls A/B/C corroboration; adjudication restores 2/3 after the copy edges are inspected. Those invented ratings illustrate disagreement resolution, not measured inter-rater reliability or model capability. If the source locator, registry coverage, or contradiction cannot be checked, hold the claim rather than accepting fluent machine text as new evidence [@official_odni_icd_203; @official_odni_icd_206; @sec:analytic-workflow].
