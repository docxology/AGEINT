### structured analytic judgment practice artifact: recurring packet and retained evidence

**Navigation.** Parent section: [@sec:part-epistemic-rigor-and-analytic-tradecraft].

The recurring practice artifact is a **competing-hypotheses evidence table** that draws on hypothesis table, assumption register, dissent field, likelihood-confidence boundary, and SAT evidence caveat. The unit keeps its learning spine explicit. Classroom packet: fictional supplier notices show a two-day delay, a weather closure on the relevant route, and a copied rumor of financial distress; no accounts are supplied.

1. Compare weather disruption, ordinary scheduling friction, and financial distress in a hypothesis table; mark which entries are observations and which are assumptions.
2. Test each item for diagnostic value rather than tallying support. Link the rumor to its single origin and record dissent about route coverage.
3. Remove the rumor: distress must lose its only direct allegation. Remove the weather notice separately: the preferred explanation must become less secure.
4. Write a provisional judgment favoring weather disruption with limited evidentiary confidence; distress remains unresolved, not disproved. Any forecast likelihood must name an event and horizon, separate from confidence; calibration requires later scored outcomes, not this one fixture.
Stop a firm attribution or numerical forecast unsupported by the packet; hand the route-coverage gap and dissent to a reviewer.

### structured analytic judgment safety boundary: accountable, synthetic, and evidence-bounded limits

No overclaiming, unsupported attribution, or bypass of reviewer challenge.

This unit introduces the part's governing question, evidence artifacts, source-support spine, and capstone thread before the individual modules begin. [@ageint237]; [@ageint266].

Learners carry one unit capstone thread through the part: define an accountable intelligence question, bind it to source-quality constraints, produce a reviewable artifact, test the artifact against failure modes, and hand it off with enough context for another analyst or instructor to audit. The capstone remains public, synthetic, or owned-lab throughout; its first source anchors are [@ageint237]; [@ageint266].

This unit's deliverables are a source-canon card, claim/evidence ledger, safe-practice lab packet, failure-mode note, instructor rubric, and debrief memo. The full source-lane and evidence-package ledgers appear in the orientation and appendices; this unit introduction keeps only the learner-facing spine for [@ageint237]; [@ageint266].

This unit's safety gates are documented authority for the exact activity, rights review, permitted data provenance, tool allowlisting, human oversight, and evidence-bounded output. A missing or unknown gate pauses the affected activity, including processing or sharing already supplied data. A written risk exception cannot waive authority or rights. A replacement tabletop may begin only after independently permitted synthetic inputs, tools and review responsibilities are established; it does not inherit permission from the stopped activity. Inspect the applicable source context: [@ageint237]; [@ageint266].

## An instructional epistemic coherence stack

AGEINT groups its defensive checks into a proposed five-layer teaching architecture, shown in [@fig:ageint-unified-epistemic-stack]: technical integrity, operational controls, structured reasoning, epistemic review, and institutional governance. The grouping is author-defined; neither the ordering nor the necessity or sufficiency of its layers has been empirically established here. Sandboxing limits permissions, provenance records describe origins, and human review challenges conclusions; none alone establishes truth. A provenance signature is not a factuality certificate, and a verifier agent may share the generating agent's sources and failure modes.

The cited epistemic-agent paper proposes a normative roadmap centered on competence, falsifiability, and epistemically virtuous behavior. Use it to formulate review questions, not as an evaluation showing that this stack protects a system's beliefs. DARPA's mixed-reality program likewise does not validate general agent assurance [@scholarly_deepmind_epistemic_agent_trust]; [@official_darpa_intrinsic_cognitive_security].

## Reliability-inspired governance: analogy and test obligation

The crosswalk [@fig:ageint-hro-governance-crosswalk] is an instructional analogy between high-reliability organizational principles and candidate agent controls. For each proposed mapping, name the failure it addresses, the observable signal, the person empowered to stop the exercise, and the synthetic negative control that would reveal a missed failure. For example, a review queue may miss a contradicted claim even when every tool call is logged; test the claim-review handoff rather than treating the presence of logs as assurance. Do not infer analytic accuracy from a reliability metaphor or a completed checklist. The judgment-correctives literature specifically challenges untested claims for analytic methods; agent red-team guidance supplies a review discipline rather than validation of this crosswalk [@scholarly_mandel_tetlock_judgment_correctives]; [@official_csa_nist_ai_agent_red_teaming_standards].
