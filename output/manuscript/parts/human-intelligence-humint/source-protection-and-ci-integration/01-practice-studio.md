#### Lesson 1: Source-Quality Anomalies: Competing Explanations and Protected

**Concept.** **Source-Quality Anomalies: Competing Explanations and Protected** is taught in the **Counterintelligence and Source-Integrity Defense** lane. The analytic distinction is to separate defensive awareness from surveillance, handling, or operational security playbooks. Apply it to the supplied evidence; a topic label alone establishes neither a mechanism nor a conclusion.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** **Completed synthetic case:** report R1 about a fictional reading-room closure says Tuesday, translated R2 says Thursday, and both cite notice N; supplied N is missing its date field. The observed discrepancy supports a source-quality concern, while translation error, stale versions, copied contamination, and deliberate alteration remain competing explanations; it does not establish penetration or anyone's intent. When an instructor supplies the complete N showing Tuesday, the reviewer corrects R2, records that an error is established but its cause is still unresolved, and sends only the case labels, contradiction, and caveat to the authorized review role. The disposition is protected review rather than accusation; no learner investigates, contacts anyone, or infers compromise from access opportunity alone, and any real identity/sensitive access or unclear authority stops the exercise. Inherited ICS/DevSecOps/SSDF citations do not validate a human-source compromise detector; absence of detected indicators is not positive report validation.

**Why it matters.** **Source-Quality Anomalies** matters in the **Counterintelligence and Source-Integrity Defense** lane because deception-aware source review and defensive escalation evidence must stay separate from judgment; source compromise is a common failure.

#### Lesson 2: Compartmentation and need-to-know governance

**Concept.** **Compartmentation and need-to-know governance** maps compartment boundaries, access lists, and need-to-know fields before any information moves across teams.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** Here agent means human source, not software agent; compartmentation limits sensitive identity/access information, but a reviewer still needs enough report-basis and caveat information to evaluate the claim. In a synthetic access card, the classroom analyst receives 'translated hearsay from notice N,' while the authorized custodian alone holds the original restricted descriptor; no identity is needed to see the shared origin. Withholding the shared-origin fact would falsely make R1/R2 look independent, while releasing the identity would add risk without improving the inference. Protection and analytic traceability must therefore be designed together rather than equating more secrecy with better validation.

**Why it matters.** **Compartmentation and need-to-know governance** connects classroom vocabulary to Counterintelligence and Source-Integrity Defense practice: learners document evidence, caveats, and reviewer ownership rather than repeating labels.

#### Lesson 3: Polygraph and Lie-Detection Claims: Physiology Is Not Truth

**Concept.** **Polygraph and Lie-Detection Claims: Physiology Is Not Truth** frames CI topics as source-vetting, anomaly review, and institutional protection—not operational entrapment.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** Polygraph measures physiological responses; a response, an inferred emotion, an inference of deception, and the accuracy of a particular report are different propositions. No appropriate empirical polygraph-validation source is supplied here, and the inherited IT-security citations do not establish screening accuracy or report truth. **Entirely invented arithmetic, not device performance:** among 1,000 fictional records with 10 stipulated positive cases, suppose a hypothetical screen flags 8 of those 10 and 99 of the other 990; only 8 of 107 flags correspond to the stipulated condition, and 2 positives are missed. This shows dependence on assumed prevalence and error rates, not real-world accuracy, and 'not flagged' is not validation. The completed decision is insufficient evidence for truth/compromise, no real testing or biometric inference, and no adverse judgment from the screen; independent report evidence and protected review remain necessary.

#### Lesson 4: Double-Agent Accounts: Defensive Deception and Source-Integrity Literacy

**Concept.** **Double-Agent Accounts: Defensive Deception and Source-Integrity Literacy** compares deception indicators with alternative explanations and uncertainty before any operational inference.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** A double-agent account concerns a human-source relationship and competing claims of allegiance or control; it is not a software-agent pattern. A report's contradiction may justify re-examining provenance, but does not establish that the source is controlled by an adversary. In the reading-room case, even a supplied correction resolving the weekday leaves intent unknown; deliberate deception would require separate positive evidence, not a label inferred from inconsistency. Read historical accounts critically for source dependence and retrospective certainty, without running an operation, testing loyalties, or tasking anyone.
