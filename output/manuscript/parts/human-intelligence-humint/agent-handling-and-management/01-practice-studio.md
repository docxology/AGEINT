#### Lesson 1: Human-Source Relationships and the Credibility of Particular Reports

**Concept.** **Human-Source Relationships and the Credibility of Particular Reports** is taught in the **Collection Management and Multi-INT Requirements Discipline** lane. The analytic distinction is to separate collection discipline concepts from operational recruitment, interception, or tasking procedures. Apply it to the supplied evidence; a topic label alone establishes neither a mechanism nor a conclusion.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** Here an agent is a human source, and a case officer/handler is a distinct professional role—not a software actor. A trusted relationship does not establish a particular report's access, accuracy, firsthand status, or freedom from translation error. **Completed synthetic lineage case:** trusted source A reports at 10:00 that a fictional seminar moved at 09:00, explicitly citing notice N1; report B at 10:20 translates A and changes 'might move' to 'moved.' The evidence table therefore records one hearsay origin, a 09:00 event claim, two report times, and a consequential translation change, not two independent confirmations; supplied original N1 at 10:30 says the move is only proposed. The final assessment retracts the confirmed-move claim, retains the proposal with caveats, and stops downstream reuse until the correction accompanies it; familiarity with A neither proves deception nor cures the error.

**Why it matters.** **Human-Source Relationships** connects classroom vocabulary to Collection Management and Multi-INT Requirements Discipline practice: learners document evidence, caveats, and reviewer ownership rather than repeating labels.

#### Lesson 5: Intermediaries: Hearsay, Transformation, and Attribution Limits

**Concept.** **Intermediaries: Hearsay, Transformation, and Attribution Limits** uses attribution indicators cautiously by separating technical similarity, context, confidence, and geopolitical inference.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** An intermediary adds a reporting or transmission step; it may introduce delay, omission, or reinterpretation without any deliberate deception. A chain of three messengers is not three independent sources if each depends on one original statement. The correct model records who directly observed the event, who relayed it, what changed, and which links are unknown, using fictional case labels rather than identities. Distance from an origin does not itself prove deniability, sponsorship, or absence of responsibility.
