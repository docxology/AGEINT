#### Lesson 4: Five Eyes Archive: Key Declassified Documents

**Worked reasoning and limits.** Completed offline chronology case: A1 is a synthetic 1946 bilateral agreement card, A2 a 1956 revision card, A3 a 2010 release notice for both, and A4 a modern summary calling them a single five-party agreement. The filled timeline assigns 1946/1956 to creation/revision and 2010 to disclosure; A4 cannot prove all partners were original signatories or that today's agency names applied then. Withheld A5 says a partner's status is recorded only in an unavailable appendix, so change its accession cell to unknown, not an assumed 1946 date. The bounded result supports successive records and a release chronology, not a present surveillance permission; stop legal inference and distinguish archive curation from independent agreement evidence (see [@sec:analytic-workflow]; [@ageint010; @ageint041; @ageint042]).

**Inputs.** For the **Declassified source-protection and institutional-control case study** scenario, use a fictional 1974 memo released in 2005 with a missing annex, a 1975 oversight extract criticizing records, and a catalogue noting selective release. **Analysis.** For **Declassified source-protection and institutional-control case study**, students first separate event, authorship, and release dates; then build a timeline from visible text and compare the later review with the contemporary memo; leave the annex blank as a control rather than inventing its contents. Mark what depends on selection or redaction and stop any causal reconstruction or modern tactical analogy that needs missing text; refer chronology disputes to the seminar reviewer. Pause whenever an inference about Declassified source-protection and institutional-control case study appears without evidence, confidence outruns support, or an agent output is treated as judgment.
