#### Lesson 1: The Free Energy Principle and Predictive Processing

**Concept.** **The Free Energy Principle and Predictive Processing** treats the free-energy principle as computational-neuroscience theory. Define the generative model, variational approximation, and observations; distinguish prediction error from variational free energy and posterior approximation from evidence that the model is true. An internally consistent update does not establish deception or factual reliability.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** A generative model p specifies how latent states s could produce observations o; q is an approximate belief distribution over those states. With p and o fixed, reducing variational free energy over q improves posterior approximation within that model, not the truth of p; prediction error is not generally identical to free energy, and information-theoretic free energy is not a direct measure of physical energy use. The worked numeric example illustrates this distinction; the inherited thesis is secondary context, while the formal sources support the mathematical account. [@scholarly_friston_2010_fep; @scholarly_buckley_2017_fep_mathematical_review]

**Why it matters.** Without explicit treatment of **The Free Energy Principle**, treating a formal theory as product evidence undermines model specification, posterior calculation, sensitivity, and theory-to-world limits review; the lesson builds the habit to separate posterior consistency under a model from evidence that the model is adequate for the world.

**Free-energy bound.** Variational free energy $F$ upper-bounds surprise $-\ln p(o)$ for observations $o$ and hidden states $s$ under a recognition density $q(s)$: $$F = D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\big(q(s)\,\|\,p(s \mid o)\big) - \ln p(o) \;\ge\; -\ln p(o).$$ For fixed model $p$ and observation $o$, minimizing $F$ over $q(s)$ approximates that model's posterior; it does not establish that the model or observation is true. The bound is not an equivalence between free energy and prediction error, nor evidence of deception detection or safe autonomous action [@scholarly_friston_2010_fep]; [@scholarly_buckley_2017_fep_mathematical_review].

#### Lesson 2: Active Inference as Computational Model of Intelligence Agent Behavior

**Concept.** **Active Inference as Computational Model of Intelligence Agent Behavior** treats active inference as an agent-modeling vocabulary: beliefs, actions, expected observations, and policy selection are classroom concepts, not proof that an intelligence agent should act autonomously.

**Worked reasoning and limits.** Separate state inference from policy selection: a posterior describes beliefs under a model, epistemic policy value concerns expected information gain, and preference-related value concerns desired outcomes. Completed synthetic case: a fictional library page is unavailable; a model favors maintenance after reading a supplied maintenance notice, but a wrong-model alternative is that the notice describes a different library. Check the approved notice's identifier and date; if they mismatch, withdraw the model-based preference and abstain on cause rather than labeling the outage hostile or taking an action.
