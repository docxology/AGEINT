### Active Inference and AGEINT conceptual primer: source context, core model, and reader task

This chapter teaches active inference as model-scoped inference and policy evaluation: observations update beliefs under explicit generative assumptions, not a guarantee of factual truth, biological equivalence, deception detection, or lawful agency. The chapter uses **Active-Inference Boundary Lens** to connect definitions, evidence tests, practice artifacts, and review gates for **The Free Energy Principle and Predictive Processing; Active Inference as Computational Model of Intelligence Agent Behavior**.

The central distinction is to separate posterior consistency under a model from evidence that the model is adequate for the world. Core topics include **The Free Energy Principle and Predictive Processing; Active Inference as Computational Model of Intelligence Agent Behavior; Shared Protentions in Multi-Agent Active Inference**. Each topic covers meaning, evidentiary support, common misconceptions, and safety boundaries.

Governance requirements use verified official, standards, public-domain, or scholarly anchors such as [@scholarly_friston_2010_fep]; [@scholarly_buckley_2017_fep_mathematical_review]; [@scholarly_friston_2017_active_inference_process]. Technical, theoretical, or empirical statements require direct domain sources and are limited to what those sources establish. [@ageint247]; [@ageint248].

Learners move from vocabulary and the **Active-Inference Boundary Lens** distinction through topic lessons on **The Free Energy Principle and Predictive Processing** with evidence and misconception checks, then assemble a **theory-to-governance card with source, analogy limit, assumption, reviewer, and stop condition** with safety and rights gates.

### Active Inference and AGEINT learning outcomes: analytic moves, evidence duties, and transfer

- Connect **The Free Energy Principle and Predictive Processing and Active Inference as Computational Model of Intelligence Agent Behavior** to **Active-Inference Theory and Governed Agent Analogy** by naming shared vocabulary, evidence burden, and audience-facing caveats.
- Build a **theory-to-governance card with source, analogy limit, assumption, reviewer, and stop condition** that keeps observation, inference, uncertainty, source quality, reviewer decision, and refresh trigger separate.
- Apply the key distinction: separate posterior consistency under a model from evidence that the model is adequate for the world; show where an apparently useful shortcut would cross that line.
- Diagnose failure modes such as treating a formal theory as product evidence, using analogy as architecture proof, citing policy guidance for mathematical claims, and letting autonomy language outrun evaluation evidence, then write one recovery move for each failure mode that preserves the learning objective.
- Teach the defensive boundary back to a peer: active-inference material stays theoretical and classroom bounded; it does not prove autonomous action, intent detection, operational performance, or deployment without explicit authority and review.

### Active Inference and AGEINT core vocabulary: source terms, method roles, and safety limits

**Navigation.** Parent section: [@sec:chapter-active-inference-and-ageint].

| Term | Working definition |
|---|---|
| Generative model | a specified joint distribution over observations and hidden states, with assumptions that can be wrong |
| Prior | a probability distribution over states before the specified observation |
| Likelihood | probability of an observation conditional on a state and the chosen model, not probability of that state given the observation |
| Posterior | the updated state distribution conditional on the observation and model |
| Variational free energy | a model-relative objective whose gap from surprise is a non-negative divergence between an approximate and the model posterior |
| Expected free energy | a prospective policy-evaluation quantity under modeling and preference assumptions, not an observed error score or a truth certificate |
