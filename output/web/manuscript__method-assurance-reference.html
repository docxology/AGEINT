Source-construction and reporting protocol: discovery records and citation boundaries

AGEINT is a curriculum-and-assurance atlas, not a PRISMA systematic review. Where a module makes a source-construction or literature-search claim, however, the source record follows PRISMA-S as a reporting discipline: record the discovery surface, search string or prompt, retrieval date, inclusion and exclusion rule, deduplication decision, source tier, conflict-handling note, direct URL, and refresh trigger [scholarly_rethlefsen_2021_prisma_s]. Discovery systems and summaries may suggest candidate sources, but final manuscript citations must resolve to directly checked official, standards, public-domain, or scholarly records in the AGEINT source atlas.

The review gate separates claim types before citation assignment. Governance claims require official or standards anchors; technical and theoretical claims require direct domain sources; empirical capability claims require evaluation or study evidence with context and subgroup caveats. If a source only supports an analogy, lesson design, or governance rationale, the generated prose must not promote it into a benchmark, deployment claim, or proof of autonomous performance.

Claim calibration and statistical limits: artifact telemetry versus empirical evidence

AGEINT treats citation counts, generated file counts, figure counts, PDF page counts, URI-link counts, and validator outcomes as artifact telemetry. They show what the local build produced and what the verifier checked; they are not learning-effect estimates, operational-performance measures, safety guarantees, or statistical evidence that the curriculum improves real-world analytic decisions. Stronger empirical or evaluated-capability language requires a direct benchmark, study, field evaluation, incident dataset, or scholarly empirical source with the method, denominator, subgroup/context caveat, and refresh trigger visible.

The claim-calibration audit enforces that separation after manuscript generation. It scans high-risk empirical, statistical, governance, safety, visualization, artifact-readiness, and formalism language; records the citation keys and source-support strength visible in the same row; allows explicit boundary language such as “not a benchmark” or “correct the misconception”; and fails unsupported proof-language, p-values, measured-performance claims, or formalisms without citations and limitations. The companion visual [@fig:ageint-claim-calibration-and-visual-semantics] makes the control surface readable, while output/reports/claim_calibration.json and .md make the same disposition auditable by script.

This is a heuristic screen of row-level wording, citation presence, and source metadata—not a semantic claim verifier. It does not establish that a cited passage entails a claim, that sources are independent, that contradictory evidence was included, or that a proposed control works. Boundary wording can also explain why a row is allowed without validating another claim in that row. A passing report therefore leaves a human source-to-claim review duty; the worked procedure below makes that duty inspectable.

Protocol claims are version-sensitive. MCP references use the 2025-06-18 specification for interoperability language and the current MCP security best-practice page for confused-deputy, token-handling, consent, and least-privilege examples; those claims must refresh when the protocol version, authorization profile, transport guidance, or security model changes [official_model_context_protocol_specification]; [official_model_context_protocol_security_best_practices]. Memory-language claims follow the same separation rule: MCP and official agentic-AI guidance support external-memory governance, tool boundaries, logging, and retention, not cognitive-taxonomy claims about episodic, semantic, or procedural memory unless a domain source is cited for that narrower taxonomy.

Analytic workflow: question, evidence, challenge, and disposition

Use this procedure with a module’s own sources and safe-practice material. It is a proposed classroom method, not a validated accuracy improvement. Analytic standards motivate explicit sourcing, assumptions, alternatives, and uncertainty; critiques of judgment aids make evaluation of those practices necessary rather than optional [official_odni_icd_203]; [scholarly_mandel_tetlock_judgment_correctives].

  1. Frame the decision. Name the question, audience, time horizon, allowed inputs, excluded actions, and human decision owner. State what evidence would be sufficient for the classroom disposition; do not silently expand collection.
  2. Preserve observations. Record a source identifier, exact passage or data item, locator, author/publisher, event time, publication time, retrieval time, version, and any transformation or translation. Preserve the original and distinguish a supplied fixture from a verified real-world observation.
  3. Trace origins before counting support. Link a repost, quotation, syndication, model summary, and translation to their upstream observation. Different URLs, publishers, models, or source families do not establish independent evidence. Unknown dependence stays unknown.
  4. Assess fitness for this claim. Separate provenance/authenticity, source access and reliability, content credibility, relevance, temporal fit, and independence. A genuine official document may describe a policy rather than an outcome; a signed image may still be miscaptioned. Record the weakest link instead of turning heterogeneous criteria into an unexplained score.
  5. Compare explanations. Separate observation, inference, and assumption. Name credible alternatives, including ordinary error or missing context. Ask which observation would be expected under each and which next check could change the judgment. Do not count supportive rows as posterior odds. Absence is informative only where the observation opportunity, expected detection, and reporting coverage make that absence meaningful.
  6. Write uncertainty in separate fields. Event likelihood concerns the proposition; analytic confidence concerns its evidentiary basis. Give the basis, contradictions, missing information, and sensitivity to assumptions. Do not invent percentages to make a sparse case look calibrated.
  7. Challenge the record. A reviewer checks the original passages rather than just the narrative. Add a duplicate report, remove a key source, and introduce a contradictory fixture. Record whether the dependency count, judgment, and confidence change for a defensible reason. This tests the artifact and review process, not universal resistance to manipulation.
  8. Dispose and hand off. Choose accept for bounded classroom reuse, revise, hold, or reject. Retain unresolved dissent, recipient/handling limits, reviewer, next check, and refresh trigger. A human—not a generated confidence label—owns the decision. If evidence changes, version the correction, mark affected claims, and notify downstream packet owners.

Completed synthetic case: three reports are not three observations

Navigation. Parent section: [@sec:method-assurance-reference].

Question. May a classroom brief say that a fictional library’s service interruption was caused by a cyberattack? All records below are invented, offline teaching cards; no real library, account, person, or system is involved. The task is an analytic disposition, not incident attribution or response.

Evidence card Observation and provenance Relation to the causal claim
R1: supplied screenshot An undated status image says “service unavailable”; original capture time is unknown. Supports only that the image contains that message, not an attack or a current outage.
R2: supplied newsletter Quotes R1 verbatim and cites its screenshot; no separate observation is supplied. Same dependency group as R1; repetition does not independently corroborate the event.
R3: supplied maintenance notice A fixture identified as the fictional library’s notice schedules maintenance during the alleged interruption. Makes planned maintenance a credible alternative; a schedule alone does not prove what occurred.
Record field Completed entry
Claim C1 “A cyberattack caused the reported interruption”; causal, classroom brief, fixture period only.
Observation R1 contains an unavailable message; R2 repeats it; R3 schedules maintenance.
Inference The packet cannot distinguish an attack, ordinary failure, planned maintenance, or a stale screenshot.
Assumption R1 depicts the same service and period as R3; this remains unverified.
Independence R1 and R2 share one origin; R3 has a separate supplied origin but a different claim scope.
Event likelihood Not quantified: neither a defensible base rate nor discriminating incident evidence is supplied.
Analytic confidence Low for any causal attribution; high for the directly inspectable R1-to-R2 copying relation within this fixture.
Dissent and next check Preserve an alternative causal assessment if offered; request an instructor-provided time-bounded incident record, not access to a live system.
Disposition Hold C1. A human instructor may accept only the narrower statement that the supplied records leave cause unresolved.
Refresh and handoff Reopen when a dated independent incident card arrives; retain packet version, reviewer, and the list of classroom briefs that used C1.

Expected negative controls. A further copy of R1 adds no independent observation. Removing R3 removes a supported alternative but does not make an attack established. A new card showing that R1 predates the alleged event weakens even the same-period outage inference. If a reviewer still accepts C1 because “three sources agree,” revise the source-origin and support-relation fields before scoring prose quality.

Bounded agent collaboration: parallel work without manufactured consensus

Navigation. Parent section: [@sec:method-assurance-reference].

Use a single analyst or agent when the question is small or the next step depends on the previous result. For independent work, assign source-disjoint or method-distinct tasks under one common question, time horizon, allowed-input set, and output schema. Each worker returns evidence IDs and exact locators, origin/dependency groups, its narrow claim, contradictions, uncertainty, and missing inputs—not merely a confident summary.

One human integration owner deduplicates upstream observations, reconciles scope and time differences, and records disagreements before drafting the joint conclusion. Do not multiply evidentiary weight by the number of agreeing agents: shared training, prompts, retrieval, or memory may create common-mode errors. A separate reviewer must inspect the load-bearing originals and an intentionally contradictory fixture. Role separation is a proposed control, not evidence that the reviewers are independent or that multi-agent work is more accurate. Stop workers from expanding authority, writing unreviewed shared memory, or converting retrieved source instructions into tool authority; hold the handoff when a material source cannot be checked.

Decision boundaries: stop, abstain, and correct

Navigation. Parent section: [@sec:method-assurance-reference].

Stop an exercise for missing authority, private or live-target material, unapproved external action, or an unsafe physical request. Hold a material claim when a source cannot be inspected or a contradiction could reverse the decision; do not replace the missing evidence with an agent consensus. Abstention is a legitimate analytic output when it specifies the unresolved question and next discriminating check. An analytic hold is not permission to delay a real safety escalation: any real incident goes to the responsible human authority under the institution’s existing procedure.

Safety boundary: accountable, synthetic, defensive, and evidence-bounded practice

Navigation. Parent section: [@sec:method-assurance-reference].

Keep all practice accountable, synthetic, defensive, logged, reversible, and evidence-bounded. Do not convert any module into live targeting, evasion, exploitation, covert collection, manipulation, or unsafe cyber-physical action. Where a module inherits a risky source motif, treat it as a tabletop, audit, provenance, or governance exercise.
