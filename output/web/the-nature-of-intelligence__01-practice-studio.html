Lesson 1: Information, Reporting, Finished Judgment, and Decision Support

Concept. Information, Reporting, Finished Judgment, and Decision Support applies structured analytic methods with explicit alternatives, evidence tables, and calibrated confidence—not rhetorical certainty.

Worked reasoning and limits. Information is recorded content; reporting communicates observations or claims; analysis evaluates their basis and implications; finished intelligence presents bounded judgments to a decision-maker, who remains responsible for action. Completed synthetic case: bulletin B1 at 08:00 forecasts severe rain; fictional depot log L1 at 08:10 records water at one entrance. The evaluated observation is one affected entrance, not a closed depot; the assessment is that access may be disrupted, with low confidence about duration, and the product asks the logistics decision-owner to review available alternatives rather than announcing closure. At 08:30 correction L2 says the water was from a pipe: preserve L1, withdraw the storm-causation inference, and stop reusing the superseded brief; this exercises [@sec:analytic-workflow], not a measured forecast method. The inherited STIX/TAXII/ATLAS citations are interoperability context, not evidence for these definitions; the official IC explanation supports the raw/finished distinction [official_intelligence_gov_how_ic_works].

Why it matters. Analysts use Information, Reporting, Finished Judgment, and Decision Support to separate intelligence work from ordinary research by naming authority, audience, caveats, and feedback. A defensible treatment names the judgment it enables for cycle mapping and audience-aware product design review, the proof limit that collapsing reporting, inference, and judgment into one line would otherwise hide, and the reviewer accountable for challenge.

Lesson 2: The Intelligence Cycle: Iteration, Feedback, and Limits

Concept. The Intelligence Cycle: Iteration, Feedback, and Limits traces requirements through collection, processing, analysis, dissemination, and feedback while naming authority and audience at each transition.

Worked reasoning and limits. The official six-stage account names planning, collection, processing, analysis, dissemination, and evaluation, but explicitly makes evaluation ongoing rather than a final quality stamp [official_intelligence_gov_how_ic_works]. A new question can emerge during analysis, and a correction can require withdrawal after dissemination; arrows therefore represent dependencies and feedback, not a universal sequence or permission to collect. Diagnostic question: does an unfilled requirement authorize new collection? No: it records an evidence gap; purpose, applicable authority, and an accountable decision are separate prerequisites. ICD analytic standards support reasoning duties, not the empirical superiority of a particular cycle diagram.

Why it matters. The Intelligence Cycle connects classroom vocabulary to Governed Intelligence Cycle and Dissemination Architecture practice: learners document evidence, caveats, and reviewer ownership rather than repeating labels.

Lesson 3: Strategic, Operational, Tactical Uses and Technical Specialties

Concept. Strategic, Operational, Tactical Uses and Technical Specialties applies structured analytic methods with explicit alternatives, evidence tables, and calibrated confidence—not rhetorical certainty.

Worked reasoning and limits. Strategic, operational, and tactical describe decision scope, planning horizon, and intended use, not fixed file formats or guaranteed time intervals. The same synthetic depot observation could inform long-term resilience investment, next-week distribution planning, or today’s access decision; each product needs different uncertainty and timeliness statements. Technical intelligence describes subject matter or specialized analysis and can support all three levels, so it is not simply a mutually exclusive fourth rung. Inherited threat-sharing standards do not define these planning levels; use doctrine as bounded terminology rather than as proof that one level is more reliable [official_joint_pub_2_0].

Lesson 4: Intelligence as Social and Epistemological Practice

Concept. Intelligence as Social and Epistemological Practice applies structured analytic methods with explicit alternatives, evidence tables, and calibrated confidence—not rhetorical certainty.

Worked reasoning and limits. An epistemic judgment asks what is warranted by evidence; a social practice also asks whose reports are heard, how translation and institutional incentives shape them, and who can contest the conclusion. Agreement among analysts is not independent corroboration if everyone read the same bulletin. In the depot case, a manager’s endorsement changes the institutional status of the brief but adds no observation about the entrance; the model answer keeps authority, evidence, and confidence separate. The inherited philosophical account supplies a perspective, not a tested algorithm for eliminating bias.

Lesson 5: National, Corporate, and Private Intelligence: Purpose and Authority

Concept. National, Corporate, and Private Intelligence: Purpose and Authority applies structured analytic methods with explicit alternatives, evidence tables, and calibrated confidence—not rhetorical certainty.

Worked reasoning and limits. Compare purpose, audience, data-access basis, and accountability separately: national work serves public mandates and institution-specific oversight; corporate analysis supports organizational decisions; private research serves an agreed client purpose. Those descriptions do not transfer state powers, restricted access, secrecy privileges, or surveillance authority to a company, contractor, or student. Public availability is not blanket permission for profiling or reuse, and a contract or consent does not remove other legal duties. For a supplied fictional market report, contractual permission to read the report supports that classroom use only; uncertainty about reuse of underlying personal records requires a hold, not an inference of government-like authority.

Lesson 6: Law, Executive Instruments, Analytic Standards, and Ethics

Concept. Law, Executive Instruments, Analytic Standards, and Ethics applies structured analytic methods with explicit alternatives, evidence tables, and calibrated confidence—not rhetorical certainty.

Worked reasoning and limits. These are different instrument types: FISA is a U.S. statutory framework; EO 12333 is an executive instrument; ICD 203 sets analytic standards; an ethical recommendation concerns justified conduct and can demand restraint beyond a legal minimum. None is a universal allied-law code, and analytic objectivity or classroom approval creates no collection authority. Record jurisdiction, instrument version/date, applicable activity, responsible approver, and unresolved scope before drawing a legal conclusion; older DCI-era EO text must be labeled historical rather than treated as a current consolidated instrument. With no applicable dated authority supplied, the correct classroom disposition is no new collection and referral to qualified review, not a claim that the named laws either permit or forbid every case.

Lesson 7: Active Inference as a Proposed Modeling Perspective

Concept. Active Inference as a Proposed Modeling Perspective treats active inference as an agent-modeling vocabulary: beliefs, actions, expected observations, and policy selection are classroom concepts, not proof that an intelligence agent should act autonomously.

Worked reasoning and limits. Active inference is presented here as a theoretical modeling perspective and a limited analyst-update analogy, not an established unifying method or evidence of intelligence effectiveness. The cited organizational application distinguishes repetitive work from the harder one-of-a-kind project setting; that boundary matters when observations do not recur under a stable model [ageint003]. In the depot example, revising a storm explanation after the pipe correction illustrates belief revision, but supplies neither a fitted generative model nor evidence that free-energy optimization improved the judgment. A classroom analogy must stop where its assumptions about recurrence, observations, or model adequacy fail.

Worked reasoning and limits. AI may assist translation, triage, retrieval, and drafting, but a proposed use is not a measured improvement in accuracy, timeliness, cost, or rights protection. Translation can erase qualifiers, retrieval can multiply one source into apparent corroboration, and fluent drafting can conceal an unsupported implication. The inherited podcast and commentary support attributed forecasts, not the universal premise that every phase is already transformed; related summaries of the same professional essay are not independent evidence. A defensible comparison would need a specified task, baseline, error analysis, and consequential limitations; absent those, retain the forecast as untested and keep the accountable analyst’s decision separate.

The Nature of Intelligence answer quality rubric: source evidence, uncertainty, and safe transfer

Judge answers with the canonical mastery evidence standard in the shared method-and-assurance reference ([@sec:method-assurance-reference]): a strong answer uses source evidence, distinguishes observation from judgment, names uncertainty, and states the safe boundary, while a revise-level answer gives a memorized definition of Information, Reporting, Finished Judgment, and Decision Support without source evidence, uncertainty, or a safe transfer task.
