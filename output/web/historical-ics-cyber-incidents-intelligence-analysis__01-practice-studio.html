Lesson 1: Evaluation Framework for ICS Cyber Incidents (ScienceDirect 2021)

Concept. Evaluation Framework for ICS Cyber Incidents (ScienceDirect 2021) is taught in the ICS/OT Cyber-Physical Defense and Tabletop Readiness lane. The analytic distinction is to separate cyber analysis from unsafe process control or live operational action. Apply it to the supplied evidence; a topic label alone establishes neither a mechanism nor a conclusion.

Worked reasoning and limits. A comparative incident framework organizes reported evidence; it cannot turn selected public cases into a representative frequency estimate. The cited paper’s full methodology was unavailable in this review, so its exact scoring formula and validation remain unverified [ageint217]. For a completed synthetic timeline, card A says ‘09:00 malware reported on an office computer’ and card B says ‘09:05 service interruption’; arranging them chronologically establishes sequence, not causation. Card C then identifies a planned outage starting at 08:55, so the reviewer withdraws the draft cyber-caused-outage conclusion, records the alternative, and stops attribution pending independent causal evidence, using Section [@sec:analytic-workflow].

Why it matters. Analysts use Evaluation Framework to separate cyber analysis from unsafe process control or live operational action. A defensible treatment names the judgment it enables for safety-aware tabletop readiness and defensive coverage review, the proof limit that IT-first assumptions would otherwise hide, and the reviewer accountable for challenge.

Lesson 4: BlackEnergy: Ukraine Power Grid Attacks (2015)

Concept. BlackEnergy: Ukraine Power Grid Attacks (2015) reads the outage case through engineering state, operator decisions, and recovery evidence in a synthetic tabletop.

Worked reasoning and limits. Honeywell describes the 2015 campaign as coordinated activity involving remote access, credential misuse, destructive components, and interference with response; the malware family name is shorthand for the campaign, not a complete causal account [ageint218]. Lesson 6: Triton/Trisis: Safety Instrumented System Attack

Concept. Triton/Trisis: Safety Instrumented System Attack focuses on safety-system integrity, operator escalation, and consequence mapping—not live process manipulation.

Worked reasoning and limits. A safety instrumented system serves a protective function distinct from ordinary process control; targeting that protection changes the potential hazard even when a public report does not establish a destructive outcome [ageint218]. Distinguish attempted impairment, confirmed modification, loss of protection, and actual harm. Neither a reported safety-system target nor a synthetic representation permits students to test, alter, or bypass any protective system.

Worked reasoning and limits. In an explicitly fictional comparison, case A has ‘simple mechanism / service interruption reported’ and case B has ‘complex mechanism / consequence unknown’; the correct output is two descriptive rows, not a numerical risk ranking. Multiplying ordinal labels has no justified quantitative meaning, and the unavailable full methodology of the cited framework prevents reproducing an exact source formula [ageint217]. The reviewer holds prioritization until consequence evidence and applicable scales are supplied; these classroom judgments are not calibrated risk or expected-loss estimates.
