Lesson 1: Declassified source-protection and institutional-control case study

Concept. Declassified source-protection and institutional-control case study studies the declassified record for institutional lessons about source protection, oversight, and the danger of translating historical methods into current practice.

Worked reasoning and limits. Use the EBSCO overview as attributed tertiary context, not a verified organizational roster or primary evidence for contested operations. A comparative service card needs date, civilian/military status, foreign/domestic remit, legal basis, oversight powers, rights consequences and archive availability; unknown cells stay unknown. Lesson 7: Stasi: Declassified source-protection and institutional-control case study

Concept. Stasi: Declassified source-protection and institutional-control case study studies the declassified record for institutional lessons about source protection, oversight, and the danger of translating historical methods into current practice.

Worked reasoning and limits. Stasi history concerns dictatorship, repression and affected persons, not merely efficient source protection; the Federal Archives describes historical reappraisal and individuals’ access to records about themselves (https://www.bundesarchiv.de/en/stasi-records-archive/). Completed offline case: S1 is a synthetic security-service file asserting a person’s disloyalty, S2 an independent dated publication contradicting its factual allegation, S3 a later archive release with redactions, and S4 an affected-person correction. The filled result is ‘the institution recorded an allegation’ (S1), not ‘the allegation is true’; S2/S4 challenge it, while S3 establishes later access but not missing content. Withheld S5 reveals S1 copied an earlier unverified file, so collapse the apparent corroboration and retract the adverse claim; stop person-level inference and retain the correction (see [@sec:analytic-workflow]). This fictional case illustrates critical reading of coercive archives, not any real person’s history or a measured account of all Stasi practice.
