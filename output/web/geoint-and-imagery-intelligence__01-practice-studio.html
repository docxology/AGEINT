Lesson 1: Imagery quality literacy: resolution, temporal fit, and uncertainty

Concept. Imagery quality literacy: resolution, temporal fit, and uncertainty treats location evidence as a quality and uncertainty problem, separating map interpretation from targeting or attribution.

Worked reasoning and limits. Imagery interpretation concerns what an image supports; mapping locates representations in a reference system; GEOINT integrates imagery and geospatial information to assess spatial relationships. Coordinates need a declared coordinate reference system, datum and uncertainty, while a sharp image can still be poorly registered. MCRP 2-10B.4 is the GEOINT support manual, not a general reconnaissance manual [ageint057]; doctrine describes a practice and does not validate a particular image interpretation.

Why it matters. Imagery quality literacy matters in the Open-Source and Geospatial Intelligence Integrity lane because source-quality review and non-sensitive geospatial interpretation evidence must stay separate from judgment; privacy drift is a common failure.

Lesson 4: Claim-ledger memory exercise that tracks evidence changes rather than people

Concept. Claim-ledger memory exercise that tracks evidence changes rather than people — Maintain claim memory as versioned source dependencies, correction history, retention decisions, and contamination checks; remembered text is not independent evidence, and this exercise does not track people or assets.

Worked reasoning and limits. In a completed synthetic scene pair with matching sensor, grid scale, processing, illumination and clear coverage, a fictional shed edge is at pixel 40 in A and 43 in B, while three fixed markers also shift by +3 pixels. Registering B by those markers removes all shed displacement, yielding ‘no resolved shed movement’; a variant with edge 46 leaves a 3-pixel residual, but a supplied ±2-pixel bound per image permits a ±4-pixel worst-case difference and the movement conclusion still stops. In a changed-feature control with edge 49, the corrected residual is 6 pixels and exceeds that bound, supporting a resolved positional difference under these assumptions, not object identity or intent. Removing markers makes registration unknown; cloud, season, sensor or resolution incompatibility likewise requires ‘not comparable’, not automatic confidence from freshness or source count. A thumbnail is no independent acquisition, publication time does not date the observed change, and two snapshots cannot establish a person’s routine. USGIF’s framework distinguishes registration, error and cross-sensor correction, but does not validate this author-defined exercise or supply a universal acceptable threshold [ageint058].
