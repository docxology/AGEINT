Lesson 1: FININT Foundations: Collection, Analysis, Reporting

Concept. FININT Foundations: Collection, Analysis, Reporting reads financial intelligence as due-diligence evidence that triggers review rather than proof of intent or guilt.

Worked reasoning and limits. FININT analysis separates transaction observations, entity-resolution hypotheses and claims about purpose or legality. A payment, a network edge and a beneficial-owner conclusion require different evidence; access to a public registry does not authorize unrestricted aggregation or prove the registry is complete or current. The two inherited encyclopedia links share a source family and are not two independent authorities for a financial or legal conclusion.

Why it matters. FININT Foundations connects classroom vocabulary to Financial Intelligence and Economic-Security Due Diligence practice: learners document evidence, caveats, and reviewer ownership rather than repeating labels.

Lesson 2: Financial due-diligence typology exercise using synthetic records and compliance boundaries

Concept. Financial due-diligence typology exercise using synthetic records and compliance boundaries structures financial-pattern review as typology-driven due diligence with escalation thresholds, not proof of wrongdoing.

Worked reasoning and limits. A suspicious-activity report records a reporting suspicion, not a conviction or proof that the named party committed an offence; reporting institutions and FIUs have distinct submission and analytic roles. Reporting triggers, confidentiality, eligible recipients and FIU powers depend on jurisdiction, institution type and the dated rule; the inherited encyclopedia summary cannot establish a current threshold. In a fictional applicability card, an archive extract dated 2024 covers entity class C, while a supplied later body notice dated 2026 expressly exempts C: a missing C filing cannot be treated as noncompliance under the obsolete card. This demonstrates checking effective dates, exceptions and operative body text rather than relying on stale metadata, not a claim about any real jurisdiction’s exemptions. Work only with fictional abstracts; do not obtain, disclose or file real reports.

Lesson 3: Non-state actor indicator review with attribution caution and governance boundaries

Concept. Non-state actor indicator review with attribution caution and governance boundaries uses attribution indicators cautiously by separating technical similarity, context, confidence, and geopolitical inference.

Worked reasoning and limits. A typology describes a possible pattern, not terrorist purpose, a prohibited recipient or criminal conduct; subscriptions and donations can explain repeated small payments. In a fictional packet, three 20-unit transfers on consecutive dates have no list match, while invoice I1 explains two as scheduled instalments and the third lacks context. The result is ‘two transfers explained by supplied invoices; third unresolved’, not ‘clean because no hit’ or ‘terrorist financing because repeated’; currency, time and counterparty context are still required. A list non-match cannot bypass transaction-context review, and a red flag cannot establish guilt. No interdiction, live monitoring or operational response follows from the classroom disposition.

Lesson 7: Money Laundering Detection: Financial due-diligence typology exercise using synthetic records and compliance boundaries

Concept. Money Laundering Detection: Financial due-diligence typology exercise using synthetic records and compliance boundaries uses typologies to structure review of transactional patterns, source quality, and escalation thresholds—not investigative targeting.

Worked reasoning and limits. In a completed synthetic packet, E1 (‘Orion Parts’, jurisdiction J, ID 11) and E2 (‘ORION PARTS’, jurisdiction K, ID 22) share a normalized name and address, list L1 names J/11, and transaction T supplies only the name: normalization produces two candidates, not one resolved counterparty, since a shared office address also need not imply control. The result for T is ‘name match; list applicability and ownership unknown’, with no guilt or blocking conclusion. Fictional classroom extracts dated 2026-01-01 use a same-ID ownership flag of at least 50% in J and at least 25% in K; for a separate E1-only record stating one relevant owner’s 40%, J’s stated flag is not triggered, K’s rule cannot be substituted across jurisdictions, and missing other ownership interests prevents a clearance conclusion. E2 is the negative control against name/address-only joining; changing E1’s supplied share to 60% changes J’s classroom flag only, while removing ID/jurisdiction stops classification instead of prompting a live lookup. All entities, percentages and rules are invented teaching inputs, not real legal thresholds, and neither a flag nor its absence proves beneficial ownership, sanctions status or money laundering.
