Structured Analytic Techniques (SATs) topic lessons: source-backed concepts and transfer tasks

Structured Analytic Techniques (SATs) builds analytic tradecraft as disciplined judgment under uncertainty: claims become useful only when evidence, assumptions, alternatives, confidence, and dissent remain visible. The sequence opens with CIA Tradecraft Primer: SATs for Intelligence Analysis, ICD 203 Analytic Standards: The Nine Tradecraft Standards, Psychology of Intelligence Analysis (Heuer, CIA 1999) and applies the Structured-Judgment Lens practice frame through concept, evidence, artifact, misconception, and transfer tasks.

Lesson 1: CIA Tradecraft Primer: SATs for Intelligence Analysis

Concept. CIA Tradecraft Primer: SATs for Intelligence Analysis studies the declassified record for institutional lessons about oversight, source protection, and limits on translating history into practice.

Worked reasoning and limits. Structured analytic techniques externalize parts of reasoning so assumptions, alternatives, and disagreements can be examined; their existence in doctrine does not establish universal debiasing or accuracy gains. Select an assumptions check for fragile premises, ACH for competing explanations, and alternative futures for scenario exploration rather than forcing every question into one matrix. The CIA and CDSE versions are access routes to the same primer, not independent studies of effectiveness [ageint189; ageint108].

Why it matters. Analysts use CIA Tradecraft Primer to separate reporting from inference, and inference from judgment. A defensible treatment names the judgment it enables for structured reasoning and source-integrity review, the proof limit that collapsing reporting, inference, and judgment into one line would otherwise hide, and the reviewer accountable for challenge.

Lesson 2: ICD 203 Analytic Standards: The Nine Tradecraft Standards

Concept. ICD 203 Analytic Standards: The Nine Tradecraft Standards — Calibrate confidence to evidence strength, source quality, and alternative explanations—not to rhetorical certainty.

Worked reasoning and limits. Map the nine standards to inspectable product fields: source quality/credibility to a source note; uncertainty to likelihood and separate confidence; information versus assumptions/judgments to labeled statements; alternatives to a competing-account paragraph; customer relevance to the decision question; logical argument to the evidence-to-inference chain; change/consistency to a revision history; accuracy to resolved-claim review; and effective visuals, where appropriate, to a legible evidence-linked display. These are product standards, not nine proven psychological treatments or an additive probability score. The objectivity pages provide context; the directive is the standard-setting source [official_odni_icd_203].

Why it matters. ICD 203 Analytic Standards matters in the Analytic Tradecraft and Source Integrity lane because structured reasoning and source-integrity evidence must stay separate from judgment; collapsing reporting, inference, and judgment into one line is a common failure.

Lesson 4: Structured Analytic Techniques (Heuer & Pherson, 2011)

Concept. Structured Analytic Techniques (Heuer & Pherson, 2011) uses the method to keep alternatives, disconfirming evidence, and confidence visible.

Worked reasoning and limits. A technique is a sequence of operations with a purpose and failure modes, not merely a named worksheet. State the question, source dependencies, assumptions, and what would change the answer before selecting a method; preserve an unresolved result when available evidence cannot discriminate. Treat professional handbooks as procedural support, not evidence that every technique improves every task [@ageint193; @ageint194; @sec:analytic-workflow].

Why it matters. Structured Analytic Techniques (Heuer & Pherson, 2011) connects classroom vocabulary to Analytic Tradecraft and Source Integrity practice: learners document evidence, caveats, and reviewer ownership rather than repeating labels.

Lesson 5: Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH)

Concept. Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) uses the method to keep alternatives, disconfirming evidence, and confidence visible.

Worked reasoning and limits. Completed synthetic ACH. Explain a fictional gallery closure notice with H1 planned closure, H2 publication error, H3 mixed/unknown cause. At 08:00 E1 is a screenshot saying closed, at 08:05 E1b copies it, and at 08:10 E2 is a signed schedule saying open: using C = compatible, I = inconsistent, ? = insufficiently specified, rows E1 = (C,C,?), E2 = (I,C,?); E1b is grouped with E1, not counted anew. H2 is provisionally less contradicted, but H3 is not excluded; removing E2 leaves no discrimination, so confidence is low and release of a causal attribution is held. At 08:20 a supplied card shows E2 was superseded: withdraw H2’s advantage and record unresolved alternatives. ACH cells are ordinal consistency judgments, never probabilities to sum; compatibility alone is not equal likelihood. Separately, stipulated P(E|H1)=0.8 and P(E|H2)=0.2 give LR=4 and, with prior odds 1:1, posterior odds 4:1 only under that two-hypothesis Bayesian toy model; real likelihoods, priors, reliability, dependence, and omitted explanations require justification [official_cia_tradecraft_primer; scholarly_karvetski_mandel_2020_ach_coherence].

Diagnosticity and Bayesian illustration. Ordinary ACH consistency marks are not likelihood ratios, calibrated probabilities, or a posterior calculation. Separately, for mutually exclusive hypotheses \(H_i, H_j\) with nonzero prior odds and justified likelihoods for evidence \(E\), Bayes’ rule gives \[\frac{P(H_i \mid E)}{P(H_j \mid E)} = \frac{P(E \mid H_i)}{P(E \mid H_j)} \times \frac{P(H_i)}{P(H_j)}.\] Compatibility alone does not make evidence nondiagnostic: invented likelihoods 0.8 and 0.2 are both compatible with the observation but give a ratio of 4. Equal likelihoods give a ratio of 1. Dependence, uncertain likelihoods, omitted alternatives, and unreliable reporting must be addressed; do not add ordinal cells or repeatedly update on copied reports. This identity is not proof that ACH improves accuracy [scholarly_karvetski_mandel_2020_ach_coherence]; [scholarly_mandel_karvetski_dhami_2018_boosting_accuracy].

Concept. Devil’s Advocacy and Red Teaming structures evaluation as misuse-case testing with blocked outcomes, logged evidence, and reviewer disposition.

Concept. Alternative Futures Analysis uses structured ideation to widen alternatives before converging on evidence-tested judgments.

Lesson 9: Deception Hypotheses: Competing Accounts and Attribution Limits

Concept. Deception Hypotheses: Competing Accounts and Attribution Limits uses attribution indicators cautiously by separating technical similarity, context, confidence, and geopolitical inference.

Worked reasoning and limits. A deception hypothesis asks whether evidence could have been selected or altered, but must compete with mistake, outdated information, and genuine disagreement. Inconsistency is a reason to examine provenance, not proof of deliberate deception; apparent motive and opportunity do not establish that an act occurred. Use only supplied documents, with no interpersonal truth tests, physiological inference, covert contact, or forced attribution [ageint193].

Why it matters. Deception Hypotheses connects classroom vocabulary to Analytic Tradecraft and Source Integrity practice: learners document evidence, caveats, and reviewer ownership rather than repeating labels.
