Lesson 1: Understanding Neurocognitive Mechanisms of Cognitive Security (Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, 2025)

Concept. Understanding Neurocognitive Mechanisms of Cognitive Security (Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, 2025) maps cognitive attack surfaces: attention, belief formation, narrative provenance, and transparent correction options.

Worked reasoning and limits. The review proposes a framework spanning executive, affective, and integrative mechanisms and explicitly says the drivers and neurophysiology are incompletely understood. A mechanism hypothesis explains a possible process; a candidate measure is an observation proposed for study; a validated biomarker requires evidence for a specified use. Publisher and PubMed records refer to the same review, not independent replications, and the framework does not validate person-level vulnerability, credibility, or diagnostic judgments [ageint176; ageint177].

Lesson 2: Executive Brain Systems: Research on Information-Related Tasks

Concept. Executive Brain Systems: Research on Information-Related Tasks connects cognitive science claims to analytic bias literacy: what the brain prioritizes, what it misses, and how review compensates.

Worked reasoning and limits. Executive functions include maintaining task goals, holding and updating information, and inhibiting an irrelevant response; this is cognitive research vocabulary, not a software vulnerability record. An error on a demanding task may reflect unclear instructions, competing demands, or unfamiliarity rather than deficient executive capacity. Neither a task mistake nor a neural signal licenses a conclusion about an individual’s susceptibility or fitness [ageint177].

Lesson 12: Decision-Making Under Uncertainty: Neural Hypotheses and Analytic Limits

Concept. Decision-Making Under Uncertainty: Neural Hypotheses and Analytic Limits focuses on transparent resilience education: provenance, audience harm, attribution caution, and non-manipulative response options.
