Lesson 1: Imagery modality literacy: resolution, scale, and sensor-fit

Concept. Imagery modality literacy: resolution, scale, and sensor-fit treats location evidence as a quality and uncertainty problem, separating map interpretation from targeting or attribution.

Worked reasoning and limits. Visible/near-infrared optical imagery commonly records reflected illumination, thermal infrared can represent emitted radiance, and active SAR measures radar backscatter with geometry-dependent effects; these distinctions are part of USGIF’s remote-sensing competency scope [ageint058]. Multispectral samples selected bands, while hyperspectral samples many relatively narrow, usually contiguous bands; neither term promises automatic material identification or finer spatial detail. Sensor modality, spatial, spectral, radiometric and temporal resolution answer different questions. In a synthetic selection card, scene A has fine spatial detail but one broad band and scene B has coarse spatial detail with many narrow bands: choose A for a supplied large-shape boundary comparison, but leave material identity unknown for both without appropriate calibration and reference evidence. The inherited NGA overview and analytic standards are not numerical sensor specifications, so unsupported performance values stay unknown.

Why it matters. Imagery modality literacy matters in the Open-Source and Geospatial Intelligence Integrity lane because source-quality review and non-sensitive geospatial interpretation evidence must stay separate from judgment; privacy drift is a common failure.

Lesson 2: Imagery collection-platform literacy: resolution and revisit tradeoffs

Concept. Imagery collection-platform literacy: resolution and revisit tradeoffs treats location evidence as a quality and uncertainty problem, separating map interpretation from targeting or attribution.

Worked reasoning and limits. Platform and sensor are separate attributes, and STIX, TAXII and ATLAS do not establish imagery-platform capabilities or collection permissions. A completed fictional media card has original O1 acquired 2025-06-01 10:00 UTC, published 2025-06-03, classroom retrieval 2026-06-08, and derivative D1 cropped/contrast-stretched from O1 with embedded metadata stripped. A supplied 2026 story reuses O1 to allege a current yard change: matching O1’s digest to its archived bytes establishes byte identity, not the current-event claim, while D1’s different bytes reflect the documented transformation rather than automatic fabrication. The output is ‘genuine older supplied scene; current change unsupported’, and missing metadata alone proves neither age nor falsity. A second copy of D1 is no independent observation; no flight planning, live collection, surveillance or real location search is performed.

Why it matters. Without explicit treatment of Imagery collection-platform literacy, privacy drift undermines source-quality review and non-sensitive geospatial interpretation review; the lesson builds the habit to separate discovery from collection expansion, and map or media interpretation from targeting.

Lesson 3: Photo-interpretation literacy: resolution, uncertainty, and scale

Concept. Photo-interpretation literacy: resolution, uncertainty, and scale — Audit imagery and map products for resolution, accuracy, temporal fitness, and uncertainty before any geospatial claim.

Worked reasoning and limits. In a completed synthetic case, scenes A and B of a fictional empty yard were acquired on 1 and 4 June but both published on 8 June; a dark region grows from 4 to 9 pixels, while the supplied illumination record changes from overhead to low-angle lighting. Aligning three fixed markers leaves the dark-region difference but cannot distinguish an added object from a longer shadow, so the output is ‘appearance changed between acquisitions; physical addition unconfirmed.’ A contrast-enhanced copy of B is the negative control: it adds no new acquisition or corroboration, and removing the illumination record increases uncertainty rather than proving construction. Stop before assigning object identity or activity, and update only if a supplied comparable scene or metadata discriminates the alternatives; a publication timestamp never dates the physical change.

Why it matters. Photo-interpretation literacy matters in the Open-Source and Geospatial Intelligence Integrity lane because source-quality review and non-sensitive geospatial interpretation evidence must stay separate from judgment; privacy drift is a common failure.

Lesson 4: Commercial imagery literacy: resolution tiers, licensing, and provenance

Concept. Commercial imagery literacy: resolution tiers, licensing, and provenance treats location evidence as a quality and uncertainty problem, separating map interpretation from targeting or attribution.

Worked reasoning and limits. Commercial availability can widen access without equalizing price, latency, licensing, technical skill or geographic coverage. A licensed scene and a public preview may have different permissible uses and resolutions, and neither permits unrestricted redistribution or analysis of real individuals. The inherited ‘democratization’ framing is a hypothesis about access, not a measured universal benefit or evidence that commercial imagery removes epistemic or legal constraints.

Why it matters. Without explicit treatment of Commercial imagery literacy, privacy drift undermines source-quality review and non-sensitive geospatial interpretation review; the lesson builds the habit to separate discovery from collection expansion, and map or media interpretation from targeting.

Lesson 5: Open-source geolocation literacy: resolution, corroboration, and uncertainty

Concept. Open-source geolocation literacy: resolution, corroboration, and uncertainty — Audit imagery and map products for resolution, accuracy, temporal fitness, and uncertainty before any geospatial claim.

Worked reasoning and limits. Geolocation compares candidate spatial explanations using independent fixed features and their uncertainty; one generic roof or road shape is not a unique match. Restrict comparison to fictional maps and supplied scenes, keep rejected candidate locations, and abstain if the available resolution admits more than one candidate. The attached NGA overview and ICD 203 do not document a specific Bellingcat methodology, so the inherited named-method attribution remains unsupported and is not an endorsement or a licence for live identity/location lookup.
