Lesson 1: Influence principles: edition-bounded recognition, not vulnerability diagnosis

Concept. Influence principles: edition-bounded recognition, not vulnerability diagnosis treats the vulnerability record as an assurance case: severity, affected component, provenance, mitigation status, and uncertainty stay separate.

Worked reasoning and limits. The seven-item formulation is edition-dependent: the author’s 2021 edition announcement identifies unity as new, establishing list identity but not independent efficacy (https://www.influenceatwork.com/announcement/the-new-revision-of-influence/); the inherited vendor history does not establish that edition or universal effects. As defensive recognition prompts: reciprocity → obligation is not authorization; commitment/consistency → recheck a changed request; social proof → popularity is not authenticity; liking → familiarity is not identity; authority → verify role independently; scarcity → urgency does not remove checks; unity → shared identity does not confer access. These are questions to ask, not deterministic weaknesses or persuasion instructions.

Why it matters. Influence principles matters in the Cognitive Security and Influence Resilience lane because information-integrity analysis and resilience education evidence must stay separate from judgment; treating resilience labels as permission to skip provenance review is a common failure.
