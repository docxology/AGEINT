Lesson 1: MICE Framework for recruitment-risk literacy

Concept. MICE Framework for recruitment-risk literacy — MICE names money, ideology, coercion/compromise, and ego as a historical motivation taxonomy. Use it to critique recruitment-risk narratives and consent boundaries, not to predict an individual’s motives or design recruitment.

Worked reasoning and limits. MICE abbreviates Money, Ideology, Coercion/Compromise, and Ego in historical/practitioner discussion of recruitment. It is descriptive vocabulary, not a validated taxonomy that assigns a person’s motive, predicts cooperation, or establishes report credibility. In a synthetic message, accepting reimbursement could reflect an ordinary expense, convenience, or many other explanations; it does not establish a ‘money motive’ or a vulnerability to exploit. Compare documentary claims about a historical case without scoring, profiling, contacting, or recruiting anyone.

Why it matters. MICE Framework matters in the Collection Management and Multi-INT Requirements Discipline lane because requirements decomposition and source-discipline fit evidence must stay separate from judgment; confusing motivation literacy with contact authorization is a common failure.

Lesson 2: RASCLS tradecraft vocabulary for recruitment-risk literacy

Concept. RASCLS tradecraft vocabulary for recruitment-risk literacy — RASCLS names reciprocity, authority, scarcity, commitment, liking, and social proof as historical influence vocabulary, whereas MICE classifies money, ideology, coercion/compromise, and ego. Neither mnemonic validates person-level motive prediction or authorizes contact, profiling, or persuasion.

Worked reasoning and limits. RASCLS names Reciprocity, Authority, Scarcity, Commitment, Liking, and Social Proof; unlike MICE’s motive categories, it organizes proposed social-influence mechanisms in a practitioner framework. Burkett’s public essay is the provenance of the MICE-to-RASCLS comparison, not an empirical demonstration that these labels reliably predict individual behavior [official_cia_burkett_rascls]. A fictional reference to an official title can be ordinary identification rather than coercive authority, and friendly wording does not establish commitment. The diagnostic answer expands both mnemonics, distinguishes their organizing purposes, and refuses person-level predictions or operational scripts; inherited ICD citations support analytic discipline, not the framework’s definitions.

Lesson 8: Shared-Experience Experiment: Closeness Is Not Commitment

Concept. Shared-Experience Experiment: Closeness Is Not Commitment is taught in the Collection Management and Multi-INT Requirements Discipline lane. The analytic distinction is to separate collection discipline concepts from operational recruitment, interception, or tasking procedures. Apply it to the supplied evidence; a topic label alone establishes neither a mechanism nor a conclusion.

Worked reasoning and limits. The directly inspected registered report tested a 2×2 shared-experience/social-connection design with final N=182 after 18 attention-check exclusions; commitment was a monetary-investment proxy for a later meeting, not actual long-term source reliability [ageint024]. Neither preregistered commitment hypothesis was supported: shared attention b=0.45, 95% CI [−2.16, 3.06], p=.734; the social-connection comparison b=0.91, 95% CI [−2.78, 4.60], p=.626. Perceived closeness/social connection increased, but this did not establish increased commitment; the authors discuss the shortfall from planned N=200 and limitations of the role-play/investment proxy. Model-answer correction: replace ‘rapport therefore commitment therefore truth’ with ‘interpersonal ratings changed; the proposed commitment extrapolations were unsupported in this study’; the null results do not prove no effect in every setting. This is research-literacy analysis only, with no replication of recruitment scripts or real-person exercise.
