Lesson 1: Declassified source-protection and institutional-control case study

Concept. Declassified source-protection and institutional-control case study studies the declassified record for institutional lessons about source protection, oversight, and the danger of translating historical methods into current practice.

Worked reasoning and limits. A third-party account hosted in CIA’s Reading Room is not thereby CIA-authored or officially endorsed. Preserve the SIS/MI6 identity while distinguishing author, genre, period and evidence for institutional roles; neither an MI5 history nor a modern organizational chart is direct evidence for SIS Cold War structure. Analyze claims and accountability without reconstructing operational tradecraft [ageint007].

Lesson 4: Five Eyes Archive: Key Declassified Documents

Concept. Five Eyes Archive: Key Declassified Documents uses the sharing arrangement to distinguish alliance governance, handling rules, legal authority, and source caveats.

Worked reasoning and limits. Completed offline chronology case: A1 is a synthetic 1946 bilateral agreement card, A2 a 1956 revision card, A3 a 2010 release notice for both, and A4 a modern summary calling them a single five-party agreement. The filled timeline assigns 1946/1956 to creation/revision and 2010 to disclosure; A4 cannot prove all partners were original signatories or that today’s agency names applied then. Withheld A5 says a partner’s status is recorded only in an unavailable appendix, so change its accession cell to unknown, not an assumed 1946 date. The bounded result supports successive records and a release chronology, not a present surveillance permission; stop legal inference and distinguish archive curation from independent agreement evidence (see [@sec:analytic-workflow]; [ageint010; ageint041; ageint042]).

Mark what depends on selection or redaction and stop any causal reconstruction or modern tactical analogy that needs missing text; refer chronology disputes to the seminar reviewer.
