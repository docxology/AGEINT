Lesson 1: Cognitive security definitions and scope

Concept. Cognitive security definitions and scope maps cognitive attack surfaces: attention, belief formation, narrative provenance, and transparent correction options.

Worked reasoning and limits. Cognitive security concerns conditions that protect informed, autonomous thinking and decision-making against information-related harms; definitions differ over whether the unit is a person, organization, or information environment. Cognitive warfare is a contested strategic/military framing, not a synonym for every cognitive-security problem: an ordinary mistaken notice does not establish an adversarial campaign. Neither term licenses deciding which opinions people may hold or treating biological/psychological/social labels as a universally validated taxonomy. The research article and its LinkedIn announcement are one underlying publication, not independent corroboration [ageint165; ageint167]; compare definitional scope rather than counting URLs.

Why it matters. Analysts use Cognitive security definitions to separate analysis of influence from persuasion design. A defensible treatment names the judgment it enables for information-integrity analysis and resilience education review, the proof limit that treating resilience labels as permission to skip provenance review would otherwise hide, and the reviewer accountable for challenge.

Concept. Comprehensive Definition: State and Process Against Malign Influence protects knowledge production with provenance, dissent channels, and transparent correction—not narrative control.

Worked reasoning and limits. A state of cognitive security describes a desired condition; a process describes ongoing source checking, correction, and rights-preserving response. Calling an influence malign requires evidence of the relevant conduct and intent, not merely disagreement or an emotional reaction. Lesson 3: Cognitive Security vs. Cybersecurity vs. Information Security

Concept. Cognitive Security vs. Cybersecurity vs. Information Security maps cognitive attack surfaces: attention, belief formation, narrative provenance, and transparent correction options.

Worked reasoning and limits. Information security protects information’s confidentiality, integrity, and availability; cybersecurity addresses risks in digital systems; cognitive security asks how information conditions affect judgment and autonomy. A technically intact message can still be misleading, while a mistaken message need not result from a cyber intrusion. Epistemic security additionally concerns institutions and practices through which knowledge is produced, checked, and distributed [ageint173; ageint175]; these scopes overlap without being interchangeable.
