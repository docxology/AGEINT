Lesson 1: Information warfare: historical policy arguments and theory

Concept. Information warfare: historical policy arguments and theory analyzes information warfare through narrative provenance, audience harm, and transparent resilience—not influence operations design.

Worked reasoning and limits. An Air Force-hosted policy study and a scholarly argument are different from adopted doctrine or current authorization. Identify author, date, genre and scope before comparing their definitions; distinguish public information duties, military information activities and the curriculum’s analysis-only boundary [ageint101; ageint102].

Why it matters. Without explicit treatment of Information warfare, treating resilience labels as permission to skip provenance review undermines information-integrity analysis and resilience education review; the lesson builds the habit to separate analysis of influence from persuasion design.

Lesson 5: Epistemic Chaos as a hypothesized adversarial goal

Concept. Epistemic Chaos as a hypothesized adversarial goal protects knowledge production with provenance, dissent channels, and transparent correction—not narrative control.

Worked reasoning and limits. Confusion can result from error, incomplete evidence or ordinary disagreement as well as deliberate activity. A conceptual description of a possible objective does not establish a particular actor’s motive, coordination or success; keep activity, intent, sponsor and impact as separate claims [ageint095; ageint096].

Why it matters. Analysts use Epistemic Chaos to separate analysis of influence from persuasion design. A defensible treatment names the judgment it enables for information-integrity analysis and resilience education review, the proof limit that treating resilience labels as permission to skip provenance review would otherwise hide, and the reviewer accountable for challenge.

Lesson 6: Active Inference and Predictive Processing as Models for Cognitive Attack/Defense

Concept. Active Inference and Predictive Processing as Models for Cognitive Attack/Defense treats the free-energy principle as computational-neuroscience theory. Define the generative model, variational approximation, and observations; distinguish prediction error from variational free energy and posterior approximation from evidence that the model is true. An internally consistent update does not establish deception or factual reliability.

Worked reasoning and limits. Active inference supplies a formal modeling vocabulary for beliefs, observations and action under explicit assumptions; a model fit does not uniquely identify a human mechanism or hostile influence. The undergraduate review and broader theoretical material do not validate a cognitive-attack detector, an individual cognitive score or a human/agent isomorphism. Compare model predictions with alternatives rather than diagnose real people [ageint106; ageint003].

Why it matters. Analysts use Active Inference to separate analysis of influence from persuasion design. A defensible treatment names the judgment it enables for information-integrity analysis and resilience education review, the proof limit that treating resilience labels as permission to skip provenance review would otherwise hide, and the reviewer accountable for challenge.

Worked reasoning and limits. The preprint proposes a construct and computational models, including a fit to empirical illusory-truth data; that grounding is not field-tested intervention assurance, and veracity judgment, sharing intention, observed sharing and task action remain distinct [ageint107]. Completed offline case: synthetic source cards mark A/B true and C/D false; a supplied learner response accepts A/C and rejects B/D, so discernment is 2/4, while separate intended-sharing responses name A/B/C and no sharing is observed. The filled conclusion is packet-specific classification only, with no causal effect, actor attribution or psychological diagnosis; stop at that boundary and preserve the correction (see [@sec:analytic-workflow]).
