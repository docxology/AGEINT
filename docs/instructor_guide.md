# Instructor Guide: facilitation flow, artifacts, and review gates

AGEINT modules are bounded studios, not operational playbooks. Begin with a
single reviewable claim; learners do not need to survey the atlas first. In the
generated manuscript, follow **Reader paths** (`sec:reader-paths`) for the
proposed 60–90-minute route and **Analytic workflow** (`sec:analytic-workflow`)
for the eight-step procedure, reusable claim record, and completed synthetic
case. The schedule is proposed teaching guidance, not evidence of learning
effectiveness. Agent use is optional; manual work meets the same criteria.

## Facilitation flow: prepare, run, pause, and debrief the studio

1. **Prepare the packet.** Use the question and initial cards from the completed
   synthetic case in `sec:analytic-workflow`, with their locators, versions, and
   origin information. Supply a blank claim record, permitted audience,
   excluded-action list, and the criteria below. Separate the completed answer
   from learner materials so it can support debrief rather than copying.
2. **Prepare an announced but withheld challenge.** Tell learners that another
   synthetic card will arrive after their first judgment. Withhold a card stating
   that the supplied status image predates the alleged event; this challenges the
   same-period inference without claiming to establish a cause. Release it only
   after the initial record is saved. The linked case supplies the answer
   pattern and duplicate/removal controls; do not substitute a surprise real-world
   incident or deception about the exercise's purpose.
3. **Assign human roles.** Name a facilitator, analyst, and reviewer. The reviewer
   must not be the author of the record being accepted. In pairs, learners can
   exchange records; a solo learner can draft independently and receive later
   instructor or peer review before classroom reuse. An agent's critique does
   not meet the independent human-review requirement.
4. **Run the proposed 75-minute route.** Allow 5 minutes for scope, 10 for source
   inspection and origin mapping, 20 for drafting, 10 for peer challenge, 15 for
   the withheld card and negative controls, and 15 for debrief and disposition.
   Shorten drafting or extend review to fit 60–90 minutes; allow accommodations
   and breaks without penalizing speed. No timed competition is required.
5. **Pause at the boundary.** Use only supplied synthetic records. Stop unsafe
   requests; hold claims whose sources cannot be inspected or whose unresolved
   contradictions could reverse the judgment. Neither consensus nor missing
   contrary evidence licenses an unsupported conclusion.
6. **Debrief and dispose.** Compare the initial and revised records. Ask which
   observation mattered, which reports shared an origin, which alternative
   remained credible, and what new observation would discriminate. The human
   reviewer records accept for bounded classroom reuse, revise, hold, or reject,
   with rationale, allowed audience, owner, and refresh trigger. A defensible
   abstention can be a strong answer. Preserve dissent and correction history.

## Diagnostic feedback: inspect reasoning rather than polish

Give a specific location, diagnosis, and next action for each material defect.
Use these prompts alongside the competency rubric in the method reference:

| Diagnostic observation | Feedback and revision task |
|---|---|
| Copies are counted as independent corroboration. | Identify the shared upstream origin; redraw the dependency map and reassess confidence without counting repetition as new observation. |
| A source is cited but the proposition is broader than its passage. | Mark the exact passage and explain whether it supports, contradicts, contextualizes, or cannot determine the claim; narrow or hold the claim. |
| Guidance is used as proof of effectiveness. | Separate the normative requirement from the empirical outcome claim; identify what evaluation evidence would be needed for the latter. |
| An alternative is named but not tested. | State the observation that would distinguish it from the favored explanation and whether the packet contains that observation. |
| The withheld contradiction changes nothing, or triggers an unexplained reversal. | Compare both record versions; explain its actual scope and revise only affected judgments. |
| A polished narrative hides unsupported certainty or an unsafe action. | Return it for evidence or safety correction; writing quality cannot compensate. |

Do not grade by citation count, publisher diversity, fluency, agent use, or
agreement with a preferred verdict. Assess whether another human can reproduce
the source-to-claim link, trace dependencies, understand uncertainty and dissent,
and see a justified response to challenge. A green claim-calibration audit is
only a screening result; it does not establish entailment or source independence.
Do not treat a classroom negative-control result as measured agent robustness.

## Access and scale: equivalent participation without mandatory tools

Offer selectable text, large-print or read-aloud cards, text descriptions of
figures, and written, spoken, or dictated claim records. Provide explicit
vocabulary support and flexible pacing. Learners may use printed cards and a
paper ledger for the complete offline/manual route: no account, network, paid
tool, or agent is required. If agent assistance is chosen, declare its role and
retain relevant outputs under the course's privacy and retention rules; never
upload personal or restricted material.

A single session produces one reviewed packet. For a module, add its domain
sources, studio task, and governance boundary to that packet rather than
restarting generic paperwork. For a unit capstone, integrate reviewed module
claims, inspect shared dependencies across them, and obtain a distinct human
reuse disposition. Do not promote unresolved claims simply because they recur.

## Required artifacts: source cards, rubrics, debriefs, and retained evidence

For a first session, retain one compact, versioned packet containing:

- Question, permitted audience, allowed inputs, and excluded actions.
- Source-origin map with exact locators and dependency groups.
- Claim record separating observation, inference, assumptions, alternatives,
  likelihood, confidence, and unresolved questions.
- Initial/revised versions and a challenge log for the withheld card and controls.
- Diagnostic feedback, learner revision, and independent human disposition.
- Review owner, refresh trigger, and correction/recipient record if reused.

For a module or capstone, extend this same packet with the authority card,
safe-lab boundary, any tool allowlist, and relevant rights, retention, and rubric
evidence. Collect what the task needs; avoid redundant forms and unnecessary
personal data.

No lab should involve live targets, private data, unsafe cyber-physical action,
external deployment, unauthorized collection, exploitation, deception, or
covert influence.
